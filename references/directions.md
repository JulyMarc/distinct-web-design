# Art-direction deck

Each direction is a real design tradition, not a style word. Pick by the brief's subject and
audience; then adapt it with the subject's materials. Never apply the same direction to two
consecutive projects without a brief-driven reason. Font names refer to `fonts.md`.

## 1. Swiss International (Müller-Brockmann, Hofmann, Ruder)
- Fits: engineering, infrastructure, B2B tools, data products, institutions.
- Type: one neo-grotesk/grotesk in 2–3 weights, large size contrasts; flush-left, ragged-right.
- Grid: strict multi-column, strong asymmetry, generous white field, baseline grid.
- Colour: white/near-white + black + one signal colour (red is classic — choose by subject).
- Devices: rules, numbers as structure, photography in rectangles on the grid.
- Trap: becoming a generic minimal SaaS page — keep the asymmetric grid and scale contrast.

## 2. Dutch structural (Wim Crouwel, Total Design, Experimental Jetset)
- Fits: tech, transport, culture, systems with modular content.
- Type: geometric or unit-based display used structurally, plain grotesk for text.
- Grid: visible modular grid; layout derived from a unit/module.
- Colour: few flat colours with systematic meaning (e.g. one per category).
- Devices: schedules, matrices, grid lines as information.
- Trap: over-decorating with grid lines that do not carry data.

## 3. Corporate modernism (Paul Rand IBM, Vignelli, Chermayeff & Geismar)
- Fits: established companies, industrial manufacturers, B2B with long history.
- Type: classic grotesk or a strong serif; confident, few sizes.
- Grid: clear, calm; big identity moments (mark, colour field) and quiet text pages.
- Colour: one ownable brand colour used in large fields; everything else neutral.
- Devices: the mark used as a graphic system, bold colour blocks, product photography.
- Trap: sterile — make the colour field and mark do real work.

## 4. Technical manual / industrial catalogue
- Fits: engineering services, field work (commissioning, MRO), hardware, spare parts, specs.
- Type: sturdy grotesk or DIN-like face; mono only for codes/specs; tabular numbers.
- Grid: dense documentation grid; tables, specification blocks, callouts, figure numbers.
- Colour: paper white or blueprint ground, black, one safety/signal colour from the field
  (safety orange, signal yellow, tag red) — used as on real equipment.
- Devices: line drawings, exploded views, part numbers, tag labels, checklists, revision marks.
- Trap: costume — use these devices for real information (specs, steps, standards).

## 5. Editorial magazine (not broadsheet pastiche)
- Fits: long-form content, research, publications, personal essays.
- Type: a text serif with real character + a contrasting display; generous measure control.
- Grid: varied column widths, pull quotes, big images with captions, real hierarchy of stories.
- Colour: mostly typographic; one accent from the publication's identity.
- Devices: drop caps (sparingly), captions, image sequences, footnotes/sidenotes.
- Trap: newspaper clichés (datelines, rules everywhere, small caps everywhere).

## 6. Japanese restraint (Hara Kenya / MUJI, Tanaka Ikko)
- Fits: craft products, calm services, health, architecture, objects.
- Type: light, quiet sans or mincho-like serif; small sizes, lots of air.
- Grid: large empty fields (ma), tiny precise text, objects photographed on plain grounds.
- Colour: material colours — paper, wood, linen, stone; almost no accent.
- Trap: generic "minimal beige" — anchor in the real material and product photography.

## 7. Bauhaus / constructivist (Bayer, Moholy-Nagy, Rodchenko, Lissitzky)
- Fits: education, culture, bold launches, posters, events.
- Type: geometric sans, heavy weights, diagonal or rotated text used rarely and meaningfully.
- Grid: dynamic diagonals, overlapping planes, strong primary colours.
- Colour: red/black/cream or primaries — choose with care, it easily becomes costume.
- Trap: pastiche; keep it legible and content-led.

## 8. Postmodern / New Wave (Weingart, April Greiman, Memphis)
- Fits: creative studios, music, youth brands, events.
- Type: mixed sizes and weights, layered type, playful spacing.
- Grid: deliberately broken; collage; patterns.
- Colour: unexpected combinations with intent.
- Trap: noise; needs a strong hierarchy underneath.

## 9. Scandinavian functional (Norwegian/Danish public design, Stockholm Design Lab)
- Fits: public services, fintech, healthcare, utilities, Danish/Nordic audiences.
- Type: humane grotesk, friendly but plain; large readable body.
- Grid: simple, accessible, generous; clear forms and states.
- Colour: soft natural palette with one confident identity colour.
- Trap: blandness — give it one ownable element (colour, illustration style, wordmark).

## 10. Brutalist web (honest HTML)
- Fits: developer tools, art projects, manifestos, indie products with attitude.
- Type: system-adjacent or raw serif/mono used with intent; default-looking but controlled.
- Grid: document flow, visible structure, minimal CSS, borders as structure.
- Colour: very few; underline links; high contrast.
- Trap: ugly-on-purpose without usability — keep spacing, measure and states correct.

## 11. Data-dense instrument (Tufte, Bloomberg terminal, timetables)
- Fits: analytics, monitoring, trading, job/market data, dashboards.
- Type: compact grotesk with tabular numerals; mono only for codes.
- Grid: tables, small multiples, sparklines; maximise data-ink, minimise chrome.
- Colour: neutral ground; colour only to encode data states.
- Trap: the "dark + acid green" terminal costume — use colour for meaning, not mood.

## 12. Luxury / fashion (Didone editorial, Fabien Baron)
- Fits: premium consumer goods, fashion, hospitality, jewellery.
- Type: high-contrast Didone or refined serif at large sizes, small refined sans.
- Grid: oversized imagery, large margins, few words.
- Colour: black/white + image colour; restraint.
- Trap: generic "luxury template" — needs real photography and a specific voice.

## 13. Vernacular / local signage
- Fits: local businesses, food, markets, regional services, culture.
- Type: inspired by the place's real signs, tickets, packaging (license-safe faces).
- Grid: informal but consistent; hand-made elements from the place.
- Colour: the place's actual colours (tiles, transport, packaging).
- Trap: kitsch; collect real references before designing.

## 14. Scientific / academic paper
- Fits: research tools, papers, methods sites, technical documentation, PhD/lab pages.
- Type: excellent text serif, sidenotes, small caps for section labels only if it's the tradition.
- Grid: single strong text column with margin notes (Tufte-style), figures with numbered captions.
- Colour: almost none; links and figures carry colour.
- Trap: dense walls — keep measure and spacing generous.

## Choosing
1. List 2–3 directions that fit the subject and audience.
2. For each, imagine the hero and one inner section; reject the one closest to a template.
3. State the choice + why + the subject materials it will use in DESIGN.md.
